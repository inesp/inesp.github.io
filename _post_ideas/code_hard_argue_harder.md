Things from: "How to build a successful team in a culture obsessed with lone heroes?" 
- Managers set the culture. They're the referees, through promotions, firings, and everyday comments. 2 real-life stories anchor this: the conflict-creating colleague who got promoted (rewarding jackass behavior), and the trusted senior engineer who joined and started blocking everyone's PRs over trivia, killing the team's motivation. ("Just because somebody is a good friend, doesn't mean he is a good co-worker.")
- Bad apples are measurably destructive. Felps/Mitchell/Byington on how one negative member tanks output; Pearson/Andersson/Porath on people quietly disengaging after incivility. Hostile
environments kill creativity and risk-taking; psychological safety is what actually works.
- The "balanced personalities" theory is wrong. The Carnegie Mellon / MIT collective-intelligence study found group IQ wasn't predicted by members' individual intelligence, satisfaction, or motivation, but by social sensitivity, equal speaking turns, and proportion of women. Google's Project Aristotle reached the same conclusion: psychological safety beats seniority, skill, and personality.
- Work isn't a separate robot-realm. We spend ~50% of waking hours there; pretending we have no emotions is absurd. Knowing what motivates yourself and others is a "power-up."
- The Kaulong anecdote (children splitting bananas down to 1/32) shows sharing-over-self-advantage is taught, not innate. Our individual-contributor obsession is a profit-driven cultural choice, not a law of nature.
